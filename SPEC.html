VOID CARTEL

A single-player space game: fly a ship through a seeded sector ladder, fight, and make money by moving physical cargo between stations whose prices disagree. Loot fills a hold you must fly home. Stations run live price boards, contracts, and three market brands. Everything unbanked dies with you. Combat, the long ladder, and a finale boss stay the spine. A visual pass — ship kit, station architecture, liveries — is part of the game, not a coat of paint added later.

spec v1.2 — closed, no TBDs Three.js r185 · Vite · ES modules single file · no external assets target 1080p / 60 fps floor physical cargo · boards · routes · contracts

How to read this document

Part I is the user guide. A player can read it alone and know what the game is. An implementer treats it as the promise the build must keep.

Parts II–III are the build plan and its proof — data model, formulas, pipeline, and the economy-invariant gates. A phase is done when its gates are green and it has been played, not when it compiles.

Parts IV–V are the numbers — every catalog, every combat and world constant, the decision log. This file is the only source of truth. Nothing outside it is required to build the game.

What this game is, in one paragraph

You launch from a station with a finite cargo hold and a contract on the board. You shoot, mine, and loot goods — hull alloy, coolant, crystal shards — that occupy slots. Stations in different sectors pay different prices for the same good, so the trip home is a route decision, not a victory screen. Docking opens a terminal; nothing banks itself. Die, and the hold and the unbanked purse are gone; credits already banked, modules, reputation, and market knowledge stay. Deeper sectors pay more and hit harder, on a straight ladder out to 230 000 u, ending at a three-phase boss.

Part I — User guide

1.1 Running the game

One launcher at the repository root. No paid assets, no downloads. Meshes, textures, and audio are generated at runtime. The launcher is honest before the project is scaffolded: if package.json is missing it prints that P0 creates it and exits non-zero, instead of a raw toolchain error.

# from the repository root (the directory that contains this file)
./launch.sh              # dev server (vite) and play
./launch.sh build        # production bundle into dist/
./launch.sh gates        # every gate: economy invariants + smoke + leak + perf
./launch.sh eco          # headless 500-trade simulation, prints invariant report
./launch.sh --seed 12345 # pin world, drops, and price drift
./launch.sh help
ItemDetail

Saves & logsThe launcher prints where runtime logs go before exec. Saves stay in the browser; nothing phones home.

1.3 The loop

60 seconds: launch from the home station with 40 cargo slots and one contract on the board: Deliver 6 coolant gel to Kestrel Relay — 1.6× that station's posted price, plus reputation. Sweep the Belt: shoot containers, mine crystals, kill a frigate. Loot is physical — it fills the hold, and at 36/40 you feel it. Home is close but coolant is cheap there. The Crystal Fields station two sectors out pays far more and sits past a Void full of rammer packs. You take the haul. Dock at Kestrel: the board reads coolant 214 against the 68 you remember from home. Sell, bank, pick up a second contract, buy a Warp Injector, install it in bay 2. Die on the way out and everything not banked is gone. The map is a risk curve. The hold is full of reasons to push it.

  • The return trip is the game. Deeper sectors pay more. Getting the goods home is where you spend the risk you took. There is no automatic conversion of a pickup into money.
  • Trade is a layer, not a menu. Route, market brand, contract, and hold management are four decisions per run, on top of the fighting.
  • Combat feeds the economy. Kills drop goods, breakables bias toward specific commodities, golden drones are walking bounties. The loot is goods worth carrying, not a score.

1.4 Cargo hold & commodities

  • Physical hold. Pickups fly into a manifest: 12 commodity types, scrap clusters, and consumables. Commodities stack up to the cap in §4.1; scrap stacks as one block. Base capacity is 40 slots. The Cargo Pods hangar upgrade adds 15 per level, three levels, so 55 / 70 / 90. A full hold rejects the pickup with a HUD nudge — return to market — and never deletes it silently.
  • Goods are not power. Nothing in the hold changes hull, shield, damage, or speed. Power comes from fitted module bays and hangar upgrades (§1.8, §4.5). Selling a good never makes the ship weaker, which is what keeps selling alive.
  • Each commodity has a pickup mesh and a tier color (common #b8c4cc, uncommon #4ade80, rare #38bdf8, exotic #fbbf24). The log tracks market knowledge — best price seen per commodity — not a stamp count.
  • Scrap clusters are field currency. They stack freely as one hold block and become spendable credits only when banked at a station (§1.10).

1.5 The trading network

Stations are not identical vending machines. Each is one of three brands, placed deterministically by the sector generator. Every content sector still has its guaranteed midpoint station.

RELIANCE depots logistics

White prefab boxes, green docking lights.

  • Buys every legal commodity at 90% of board. Refuses contraband.
  • Full repair, banking, module shop, consumables.
  • The reliable cash-out. Never the best price. Never a dead end.

KESTREL exchanges market

Ring-truss platforms, amber signage, docking arms, a readable price-tower hologram.

  • True board: buys at 100% of local value, sells at 130%.
  • Contracts: always three offers (§1.7).
  • Reputation perks apply here: discounts, exotic module stock.
  • Refuses contraband.

MIDNIGHT DOCKS gray

Unmarked hulls, red running lights, one airlock too many.

  • Buys anything. No contract board. +10% on contraband (Void Relic, Warden Shard, and goods flagged stolen from kills).
  • Sells bootleg modules: four curated modules, 20% cheaper, each with a named drawback.
  • Repair costs +25%, shown on the receipt.

Brand is visible on the nav ring before you commit. You always know which kind of market you are flying toward. That is the route decision.

1.6 Price boards & routes

  • Every Kestrel board, every sector. Price = commodity base × that sector's demand factor × a seeded slow drift (§2.3, ±15%, a new sample every 90 s of game time). Demand is a real table (§4.2): coolant is cheap where it is made and dear where reactors cook; crystal shards are dirt-cheap in the Crystal Fields and expensive in the Belt.
  • Routes are the meta-game. The tables produce canonical runs: Belt alloy into the Forge; Crystal Fields shards into the Belt; Plasma coolant back toward home. §3.1 proves at least three routes clear 25% net after a round trip. Arbitrage is a pass/fail line, not a vibe.
  • Market knowledge. Visiting a station records prices for commodities you have seen. The nav ring shows a delta against your last reading. The Trade Ledger module upgrades this to live remote quotes. Knowledge survives death — it is in your head, not your hold.

1.7 Contracts

  • Every Kestrel visit posts three offers, seeded: deliver N of a commodity to station X for a 1.4–1.7× multiplier on the destination's sell price, plus trade reputation. The multiplier is rolled per offer; higher reputation rolls higher. Deadlines are distance-based: at least 1.6× the flight time at 80% throttle. Never a reflex test.
  • Accepting writes the contract into the hold manifest. Delivery pays automatically on docking the destination. You still fly the trip. Declined offers refresh on the next visit, not on a reload.
  • Higher reputation unlocks larger contracts and deeper destinations. A contract is the reason to visit a sector you would otherwise skip.
  • Completing a contract also gives that station's brand a small standing bump: prices there warm by up to 6% for 10 minutes (§2.3).

1.8 Modules & outfitting

Ship power, with no hidden consumption and no passive buff from a hoard:

  • Modules are permanent purchases stored in a warehouse. Installing moves one into one of three bays. Swapping at any station is free and never destroys a unit. Uninstall returns the module to the warehouse.
  • Buffs come only from fitted bays. Warehouse stock does nothing. A module you are not flying does not change a stat.
  • Three honest states. Hold = market goods. Bays = the ship's power. Warehouse = the portfolio. The HUD shows all three separately (§1.12).
  • Ten modules (§4.3). Rare and exotic stock at Kestrel (reputation-gated) and, as bootlegs, at Midnight Docks.

1.9 Consumables

Field items that occupy cargo slots, so a repair kit costs market space:

  • Hull Patch (1): +40 hull over 2 s, interruptible. Cheap at any station. Stacks 5.
  • Shield Cell (2): instant +35 shield, resets the regen delay. Stacks 5.
  • Fuel Rod: +1 boost charge, instant. Stacks 3.
  • Distress Flare: one-shot local repair drone, 30 u, heals 4 hull/s for 10 s, draws aggro.

Slots spent on kit versus goods is the loadout decision of the trip.

  • Trade reputation is lifetime credits banked through the network. It is a rank, never a currency. Tiers: Stranger → Courier (5 000) → Freighter (20 000) → Tycoon (80 000). Perks: sell bonus +0 / +3 / +6 / +10%, better contract rolls, exotic module stock, cosmetic tiers.
  • No duplicate value, no negative balance, no hidden conversion. Credits appear only from field scrap (capped spawn), sell prices (≥ 50% of base after brand rules), contract premiums (≤ 1.7× board), and the fixed bounty table. §3.1 asserts this on every build.

1.11 Cosmetics & look

World & effects

  • Ship kit: the player ship and all nine enemy types are assembled from one shared chamfered part kit. No raw default-segment primitives. Hard-normal facets, PBR materials from procedural 512² roughness/AO textures, a per-type emissive signature so a type reads before its name does. Fitted modules light visible pods on the hull — the loadout is readable from outside.
  • Station identity: three architectures, recognizable on approach. Reliance prefab boxes. Kestrel ring-truss, docking arms, a price-tower hologram you can read from range. Midnight: a low unmarked hull and one flickering airlock.
  • Post: ACES tonemap, selective bloom on emissives only, light grain. Quality tiers 0–3 with auto-degrade. The price tower is the bloom showcase — markets are visible at distance.
  • Juice on economic moments: a kill scatters parts and magnet-swooshes into the hold with a slot tick; a sale flies a credit chip and rolls the wallet; a completed contract stamps; a rejected pickup flashes the cargo door; banking plays a manifest scan.

What you earn and buy (§4.4)

  • Liveries paint hull / trim / glow zones, patterns solid, hazard-stripe, splat, or a corporate decal sheet. Three starters are free. The rest unlock by reputation tier or cost 50–400 credits. Your livery is on the death screen and the dock camera.
  • Engine trails, weapon tracers, HUD themes, kill decals. A Sentinel kill chips that silhouette onto the nose. A Warden kill is a permanent hull scar and a title.
  • Cosmetics never gate power. They spend the same credits as modules. Flex versus firepower is a real choice only because the economy works.
  • Drydock tab at stations: a live turntable of your ship, paint chips, buy-and-equip while paused.

1.12 HUD

  • Cargo bar, bottom center: 23/40, the top-value commodity, and what it is worth at the station you are pointed at (alloy ×8 → ~640 @ Kestrel).
  • Purse chip beside it. Banked credits appear in the terminal only. Two wallets, two homes, no ambiguity.
  • Contract tracker, top right when one is accepted: goods, destination, time budget.
  • Approach panel: brand, spread, and the top three sell values for what you hold.
  • Three bay pips showing the fitted loadout.
  • Hull and shield bars, reticle color (red / orange / white by threat), boss bar, sector name, and distance from the sun. Commodity markers stay on while hauling: worth-here versus best-known, aimed at the nearest station.

1.13 Combat & the ladder

You fly away from a sun at the origin. Distance from it is the difficulty axis. Content sectors alternate with empty Voids that are deliberate breathing room. Enemy stats scale linearly, stat × (1 + 0.25 × (sector − 1)), capped at sector 6. Full tables: §4.6 and §4.7.

  • You. Hull 100 (no regen; stations repair it). Shield 50, regenerates at 10/s after 3 s without a hit. Speed 80 u/s, boost ×2. Collision radius 2.5 u. 0.75 s invulnerability after a hit.
  • Weapons. Pulse autocannon, IEM burst, missile volley — numbers in §1.2 and §4.5. Every enemy attack has a named tell: cloak shimmer, beam telegraph, charge glow, fleet weave.
  • The ladder runs 0 → 230 000 u across nine content sectors and five Voids, then THE END, where the universe drains 4 hull/s after an 8 s grace. You are not meant to live there.
  • The Warden waits in THE FORGE at 96 000 u. 3 000 hull, three phases (barrage, minefield plus drones, enrage beams). Killing it drops a Warden Shard and scars the hull.
  • Stations are rare. One guaranteed midpoint station per content sector, plus a 0.25% chance per content chunk. Safe bubble 120 u. The world spawn is a 1 200 u threat-free zone.

1.14 Additions since P8 (v1.2)

Five post-release phases, each shipped with its gate (§3.2). P9 filled the combat ladder; P10–P13 are feel-and-readability fixes driven by playing the game.

P9 — Combat finale

  • Sector-exit Sentinels: a mini-boss gates every sector exit (§4.6), spread burst, summons drones, guaranteed rare drop.
  • The Warden and sector hazards (§4.7: pulsar sweeps, debris storms) went live against their tables.

P10 — Flight camera, aim, crosshair

  • Chase camera sits behind the nose. The §4.5 offset was applied with the wrong sign: the ship rendered in front of the camera and vanished. The engine nose is −Z; updateCamera negates the offset. Verified by gate: camera→ship dot > 0.9.
  • Aim uses local axes — mouse right always yaws right under any roll.
  • Visible crosshair: ring + four ticks + center dot, threat-colored per §1.12.

P11 — Sun reference, pause menu, world density

  • The sun never disappears. It is the player's compass, so the sun mesh is re-parked every frame on the true bearing from the camera, distance capped at 4 200 u inside the far plane, frustum culling off. The real sun stays at (SUN_CORE, 0, 0); never read the mesh position as world truth. An additive halo sprite breathes so it reads as a star.
  • P pause menu: freezes the sim with the same halt semantics as the trade terminal (D13), then shows bank credits, purse at risk, cargo fill, hull/shield, sector and distance from the sun, contracts completed and reputation, the active contract, and boost charges — next to the full key map. Auto-dismisses on death or when the terminal opens; the two never stack. Decor and sun parking keep running while paused so the frozen frame stays correct.
  • Interstellar gravel: a seeded field of ~700 tetrahedra in 1 500 u cells around the ship, rebuilt on cell crossing. One instanced draw call, zero sim state, deterministic via rngStream — the Voids breathe, they no longer look empty.

P12 — Dynamic light & post effects

  • One combined finisher pass (D14) carries grain, vignette, radial chromatic aberration, boost speed lines, and the hurt flash. Frame cost stays one quad regardless of effect count; tier 0 skips it entirely so the software-GL floor stays cheap.
  • Chromatic aberration is radial: zero at the crosshair (aim stays sharp), rising toward the edges; it idles near invisible, heats with sustained fire, strongest under boost.
  • Speed lines streak radially during boost — velocity you can see.
  • Hurt flash kicks to red for ~0.2 s on damage.
  • Glow sprites feed bloom: engine glows follow throttle and flip red while boosting (the §4.4 tell), a 12-sprite muzzle pool pops in the current tracer cosmetic color, impact sparks on every confirmed hit. All pooled, zero per-frame allocation; engine glows re-parent on hull swap.

P13 — Aim is a self-centering virtual stick (D12)

  • Pointer deltas accumulate into a pull offset around the crosshair that commands turn rate — hold it and the ship keeps turning forever, no screen-edge wall. Release; after 2 s the stick eases to neutral and the ship flies straight, holding its heading. A small blue dot mirrors the stick so you always see which way you are pulling. Full rationale: §5.1 D12.

P14 — Playability pass (D15)

  • Drivable aim. Sensitivity halved (0.0022 rad/px) and shaped by a 1.6 curve: gentle near the crosshair, full authority at the edge.
  • Biome identity. Each sector palette carries instanced silhouette fields — rust boulders, crystal shards, derelict panels, graveyard plating — in per-instance biome colors. Three draw calls, zero sim state.
  • Readable enemies. Every hostile renders 1.6× its collision radius (2 u floor) with a per-type emissive signature color; crates never threat-flag the reticle.
  • Stations found again. Extra-station chance 0.02 per content chunk plus a Void waystation roll.
  • Breakables shipped. The §1.3 promise finally exists: 2–4 anchored crates per content chunk, shoot open, spill biome-biased goods. Loot despawns with its chunk (pool saturation bug found by the p14 gate).
  • Boost lost its tunnel vignette — streaks alone sell the speed.

P15 — Trade & safe zones (D16)

  • Four panels, not one wall. The terminal splits into SELL · BUY · EQUIP · SHIP, Tab/←→ cycles panels, ↑↓ rows, Enter acts. The BUY panel has an explicit quantity selector (-/+); the EQUIP panel leads with a live ship-stats bar. Every row carries a tooltip — what it does, what it costs, why it is locked.
  • Mouse freed on open. The terminal drops pointer lock when it opens and the header states "press ESC to free the mouse cursor".
  • Hangar upgrades purchasable. Cargo pods / hull plating / shield capacitor / weapon tuning / engine Mk — three levels each, permanent, sold on the SHIP panel. The stats() counters had been waiting for a shop since P5.
  • Security shields. Wireframe domes at every station, radius = the H-interaction constant. Inside: hostiles idle and ignore you; the prompt reads SHIELD SAFE, the reticle turns cyan. Leaving re-acquires the frame you cross the dome.

P16 — Graphic rehearsal (D17)

  • Measured, not eyeballed. A framed readPixels harness screenshotted every entity kind against black and counted lit pixels before/after — the numbers drove the fixes.
  • Projectiles emit light. Pulse bolts: fat capsule cores with additive halo sprites matching the tracer color; the salvo reads as a stream of comets.
  • Missiles are fireballs. Cone darts oriented to velocity, per-frame exhaust puffs, launch flashes, and an orange shockwave + fireball debris on detonation.
  • IEM is a set piece. Double cyan/white shockwave expanding to the true IEM radius — the AoE is now legible while it happens.
  • Contact ring. Hostiles (diamond in their signature color), breakable crates (green square), and loot (tier-colored dot) get screen-projected markers with distances, edge-clamped and nearest-first, the same language as the station nav ring. Behind-camera contacts drop; the ring clears inside the terminal.

Part II — Implementation

2.1 Design laws

These are invariants, not taste. A change that breaks one is a design change and needs a decision-log line, not a quiet patch.

#LawWhat it forbids
L1Pickups never become money by themselves. A collect writes the hold. Credits exist only after an explicit sell or bank at a station.Auto-sell, wallet credit on magnet, "sell above threshold".
L2The hold is what death takes. Death clears hold and purse and writes nothing into meta.A death penalty aimed at an empty manifest. Banking as a side effect of opening the menu.
L3Power comes from bays and hangar upgrades only. Warehouse stock, carried goods, and reputation do not change combat stats. Reputation changes prices, stock, and contracts.Passive buffs from a hoarded inventory. Selling a good as a loss of power.
L4Refit never destroys hardware. Install, uninstall, and swap conserve the module count. Buy adds one. Sell-for-parts is an explicit, confirmed action.Install that consumes a unit with no refund path.
L5Profit requires movement. Buy-then-sell at the same station, drift frozen, returns at most 72% of the price paid. At least three cross-sector routes clear 25% net. None exceed 3.2×.A fixed spread with no geography. A print-money route.
L6Same seed, same world, same boards. Generation, drops, brands, and drift are pure functions of the seed plus coordinates or station id.Unseeded Math.random on any of those paths.
L7The economy crate imports no renderer. src/economy/ is plain data and pure functions, runnable under Node. The sim can be proven without a GPU.A THREE import under economy/. A gate that needs a browser to check a ledger.

Why these are one design, not six patches

Turn L1 off and there is nothing to haul. Keep L1 but drop L5 and every station is the same bad deal. Keep both but let the warehouse buff the ship (breaking L3) and hoarding beats flying, so death stops mattering. The gates in §3.1 exist so the subsystem cannot silently rot back into that contradiction.

2.2 Economy model

Two stores, one direction of flow each. Both are plain data.

// meta — persisted, validated on load
meta = {
  credits,                          // integer
  warehouse: { moduleId: count },
  bays: [moduleId | null, …],       // length 3
  ships: { owned: [], active },
  upgrades: { id: level },
  rep: { lifetime, tier },
  knowledge: { commodityId: { seenAt, lastPrice, bestSeen } },
  cosmetics: { owned: [], active: { livery, trail, tracer, hudTheme } },
  contractsHistory
}

// run — one flight, forfeited on death
run = {
  hold: { itemId: count },          // commodities, scrap, consumables
  holdSlotsUsed, holdSlotsMax,      // 40 + 15 × cargoPodsLevel
  purseScrap,                       // bankable, lost on death
  contract: { offer, acceptedAt, deadlineS } | null
  // plus combat fields: hull, shield, position, cooldowns
}
  • Flow. Field → run.hold (magnet pull is unchanged: 120 u/s inside 30 u) → station terminal → an explicit SELL or BANK. Bank converts scrap clusters and completes a contract delivery. Goods convert only through the sell grid. H opens the terminal. Nothing banks because a key was pressed.
  • Module flow. BUY adds one to the warehouse. INSTALL moves it into a free bay. Swap moves. Stats are read only from bays.
  • Price state is world data, seeded from worldSeed plus station id (§2.3). It is reproducible and shared across visits.

2.3 Price model

localValue(commodity, station, t) =
    basePrice × demand(commodity, station.sector)     // §4.2, 0.55 – 2.40
            × (1 + drift(station, commodity, t))      // seeded sine noise, ±0.15, period 90 s
            × repWarm(station)                        // 1.00 – 1.06, decays over 10 min

playerSellPrice = round(localValue × brandSell)
    // KESTREL 1.00 · RELIANCE 0.90 · MIDNIGHT 1.10 on contraband, 1.00 otherwise
playerBuyPrice  = round(localValue × 1.30)
  • Contract price = destination playerSellPrice × (1.4–1.7), seeded per offer. The premium stacks on the destination board, so routes, boards, and contracts add instead of competing.
  • Brand refusal. Reliance and Kestrel will not buy contraband. Midnight will, at +10%. That is the whole rule; it is one column in §4.1.
  • Spawn guard. Field scrap is the only currency that enters from the world. Per-chunk drop counts are capped (§4.5). The invariant run asserts expected combat income stays ≤ 2.2× a sell-everything trip, so fighting never outpays hauling by an order of magnitude.
  • Every coefficient above is a const in economy/constants.js. The functions are pure and unit-tested. ./launch.sh eco simulates the market with no browser.

2.4 Trade terminal

A station terminal, about 96 vw, keyboard-navigable, pauses the simulation. Four distinct panels (D16 — one tabbed mega-grid proved too opaque):

  1. SELL · MANIFEST — the hold as rows: name, count, slots, worth-here, delta versus best known. Enter builds a slip, -/+ adjusts, footer SELL SLIP confirms. Contract offers and delivery live at the bottom of this work panel.
  2. BUY · RESOURCES — the commodity market with an explicit quantity selector (-/+ adjusts, Enter buys the quantity, B buys one). Sell/buy columns, drift sparkline, demand factor and lore in the tooltip; undiscovered rows grey until knowledge exists (§1.6).
  3. EQUIP · MODULES — a live stats bar (hull, shield + regen, DPS, speed, hold, magnet, bank) above module buy/install/uninstall/melt and consumables. Every row carries a tooltip: what the module does, what melting costs, why a rep gate locks it.
  4. SHIP · UPGRADES & HANGAR — permanent hangar upgrades (cargo pods, hull plating, shield capacitor, weapon tuning, engine Mk; three levels each, survive death), hulls (buy once, swap free), the drydock turntable and cosmetics.

Mouse release (D16): opening the terminal drops pointer lock and the header states “press ESC to free the mouse cursor” — the cursor is clickable for panel rows. ESC itself closes the terminal (same as H).

Security shields (D16): every station is sheathed in a visible wireframe dome whose radius is the H-interaction radius (STATION_BUBBLE, 120 u — one constant, no drift). Inside the bubble the ship is masked: hostiles stop closing and stop firing (they idle-drift and re-acquire the frame you leave). In-flight projectiles still land. The dock prompt reads “SHIELD SAFE” and the reticle tints cyan while masked.

BANK (footer, unchanged): scrap, contract delivery, and hull/shield repair with itemized costs; Midnight's +25% on the receipt. Nothing sells on a stray click; closing discards an unconfirmed slip; irreversible actions confirm. This is the emotional center of the loop; it is never a side effect.

The terminal is diegetic: colors, type, and hologram noise follow the station brand. A HUD theme reskins the terminal, not just a bar.

3.3 Perf & leak gates

  • Draw calls are measured around the real frame, not the post pass. renderer.info.autoReset = false, reset once per frame around composer.render(). Budget ≤ 200 draw calls in every sector including the finale. A gate that samples the output pass and reports "1" is a broken gate.
  • Death is deterministic in the harness. A debug-only __VC__.applyPlayerDeath() triggers the forfeit path. Do not race a drone into the player and hope.
  • Two baselines, labeled. A software-GL smoke floor (sanity: it runs) and a GPU budget (60 fps, 1920×1080, heaviest sector, price-tower holograms and bloom on). Never average them into one number.
  • What is sampled. performance.now() ring buffer, 60 s steady, 5 s warm-up discarded, report p99 not the mean. Leak signature: heap growth ≤ 15 MB over 5 minutes, and a restart returns to the baseline within 2 MB.
  • The terminal is DOM and pauses the sim. Board sparklines and the 3D backdrop are still budgeted: ≤ 12 ms worst-case to open, and that number is measured.
  • Hard ceilings from §4.5: 8 dynamic lights, 1 500 pooled particles, 200 pooled projectiles, device pixel ratio capped at 2, delta time capped at 0.1 s, RAF pauses when the tab is hidden.

  • Hold — the run's cargo manifest, in slots. Dies with you.
  • Purse — unbanked field scrap, inside the hold. Dies with you.
  • Credits — the persistent wallet. Only exists after banking.
  • Board — a station's live buy/sell grid, from §2.3.
  • Route — a commodity plus an origin and a destination that nets profit after the spread.
  • Brand — Reliance, Kestrel, or Midnight.
  • Bay — one of three module slots on the ship. The only source of item power.
  • Warehouse — banked modules. No passive effect, ever.
  • Knowledge — discovered prices. Survives death.
  • Contraband — a commodity tag only Midnight will buy.
  • Livery — a paint-zone cosmetic: hull, trim, glow, and a pattern.
  • Ladder — monotonic distance from the sun through the sectors in §4.7.
